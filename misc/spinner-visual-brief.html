Spinner Visual Brief

Reference doc for briefing an external design AI on two new effects replacing the Remembrance Spirit's finale: a single bouncing Spinner and its four-direction mini Arc Blade slash. Paste the boxed prompt at the bottom directly, or hand the whole page over as context.

The mechanic, in plain terms

Today, when a Remembrance Spirit reaches the end of its 35-second life, it fires 8 identical bouncing balls that scatter outward. That's being replaced by one much stronger object — a Spinner — that lives for 5 seconds:

  • It launches toward whichever direction of the screen currently has the most enemies clustered together — not a random or nearest-enemy direction.
  • It flies fast, bounces off every screen edge indefinitely, and gets a real speed rush at two moments: right after launch (fading over its first 2 seconds), and again, briefly, every time it bounces off a wall.
  • Any enemy it physically touches takes heavy true damage — and unlike a normal bouncing ball, it can hit the same enemy again after a short cooldown, not just once ever.
  • Whenever an enemy is near it, it periodically slashes out 4 small arc blades in a fixed cross pattern (up/down/left/right), on a steady beat — a secondary attack layered on top of the bouncing body.
  • When its 5 seconds run out, it's gone — no lingering, a clean despawn.

Data shape (for grounding, not for the artist to read literally)

// The Spinner itself — one object, spawned once per finale

  lastArcTick: 0,           // every 300ms, if an enemy is nearby: fire the 4-blade slash
  hitCooldowns: new Map(),  // enemy → next time it can be hit again (not "once ever")

// Each of the 4 mini arc blades it slashes out, fixed N/E/S/W directions
{

  radius: 70,             // smaller than the Spirit's own Blade Arc (radius 125)
  isSpinnerBlade: true,    // flags it as this new, visually distinct blade variant
}

Existing visual language to match — and depart from

The Remembrance Spirit's own palette

The Spirit and everything it fires reads in magenta/pink (hex family around #ff44aa) with white-hot highlights. The Spinner should clearly belong to this same creature — same family of pinks/magentas — but needs its own identity distinct from a plain bullet: think a dense, spinning, faceted mass rather than a soft glowing orb.

The Spirit's current Blade Arc (the one already in the game)

Every 5th shot, the Spirit already fires a lime-green Blade Arc: a wide half-ring stroke (like a scythe swing) with rotating pixel-square decorations and small orbiting "atom" particles drifting off its face. It's a big, deliberate arc — radius 125, built from layered strokes (soft outer wash → glow → bright core → thin edge line).

The new mini Arc Blade (the 4-directional one the Spinner fires) needs to read as a relative of that move — same "arc slash" family — but genuinely redesigned, not just a shrunk recolor: smaller (radius ~70), a different silhouette or decoration language, and ideally a color that separates it from both the big Blade Arc's lime-green and the Spinner's own magenta (a third accent — perhaps a hot white-violet or electric cyan — so all three read as distinct at a glance in a busy fight).

Quality tiers

This game auto-scales visual detail across 3 tiers based on live performance (desktop stays on the top tier almost always; the drop only happens on genuinely weak hardware or heavy on-screen clutter). Every effect needs a design for all three — the artist should describe how each one sheds detail going down the tiers, not just draw the top tier and stop.

TierWhat survives
FullFull glow/bloom, layered strokes, particle trails, rotating decorative elements — the "hero" look.
MedFlat gradient fill, no glow/shadow blur, no decorative particles — silhouette and color read the same, detail doesn't.
LowA single flat-colored shape, no gradient — must still be instantly readable as "the Spinner" / "the mini blade" at a glance from its outline and color alone.

Effects needed

LaunchThe instant the Spinner fires off from the Spirit's position — a burst/wind-up moment that sells the initial speed rush.
In-flight motionIts constant look while spinning and bouncing around the arena — the idle/traveling state, most of its 5-second life.
Speed-boost tellA visible difference during the launch ramp and each post-bounce speed spike — motion blur, trail stretch, brighter core, whatever reads as "faster right now."
Arc-blade slash launchThe moment all 4 mini blades fire outward from the Spinner.
Arc-blade modelThe blade's own shape/body while it travels outward — the new, redesigned look described above.

DespawnHow the Spinner ends at the 5-second mark — a deliberate finish, not a pop.

Ready to paste

Design the visual appearance of two new bullet-hell projectile effects for a space-shooter game, in the visual language of an existing "Remembrance Spirit" ally that fires magenta/pink energy attacks (hex family around #ff44aa).

Effect 1 — the Spinner: a single dense, fast-spinning projectile (roughly 70px diameter on screen) that launches from the Spirit toward the densest cluster of enemies, then bounces off the screen's edges for 5 seconds, visibly speeding up right after launch and again briefly each time it bounces off a wall, before a deliberate despawn. It should read as a family member of the Spirit's magenta palette, but as a dense faceted/spinning mass rather than a soft glowing orb.

Effect 2 — the mini Arc Blade: while the Spinner is near an enemy, it periodically slashes out 4 small versions of the same energy blade in a fixed cross pattern (up, down, left, right). The game already has a larger lime-green "Blade Arc" — a wide half-ring energy slash with rotating pixel-square decorations and small orbiting particle motes. This new mini blade (roughly half that size) needs to read as a clear relative of that move but genuinely redesigned, not a shrunk recolor — ideally its own third accent color (e.g. a hot white-violet or electric cyan) so it's visually distinct from both the Spirit's magenta and the big Blade Arc's lime-green in a busy fight.

Deliver both effects across 3 graphics quality tiers: Full (complete glow/bloom, layered detail, particle trails), Med (flat gradient shapes only, no glow or particles), and Low (a single flat silhouette, still instantly recognizable by shape and color alone). For each effect, separately describe: its launch moment, its idle/in-flight look, a visible "speeding up" state, an on-hit impact flash, and (for the Spinner) its despawn moment.
